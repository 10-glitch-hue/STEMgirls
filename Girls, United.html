Welcome to Girls, United!

In a world where STEM fields are predominantly male, there's a need for a safe space where women all over the world can connect and empower one another. Girls, United is a student-made website prototype that is meant to serve as a platform for learning and encouraging young girls and women to pursue STEM. From indepth explanations, to resourceful researches and quizzes, Girls, United facilitates learning for girls all over the world!

Africa's milestone!

Africa now produces a higher share of women STEM graduates than any other region in the world! The study shows that 47% of science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) graduates from African universities are women, surpassing Europe’s 42%, Asia’s 41%, and North America’s 39%...

A win for Kuwaiti women

Kuwaiti women excel in economy, diplomacy, and national leadership. Assistant Foreign Minister Ambassador Sheikha Jawaher Ibrahim Al-Duaij Al-Sabah affirmed on Wednesday that Kuwait ranks among the highest in the world for women's empowerment in science and technology, achieving a rate of 70 percent...

A Doctor's journey

Thailand outperforms many countries in gender representation in STEM. According to the latest data from the UNESCO Institute for Statistics, 45.9 per cent of researchers in Thailand are women—significantly higher than the global average. Yet, for Dr Pongkarn Chakthranont, a researcher at the National Nanotechnology Center and fellow 2024 L’Oréal-UNESCO research grant awardee, exposure to different academic environments revealed a stark contrast...

New inventors!

The 2025 L'Oréal USA For Women in Science Awardees have just been released! The US program is part of the global L’Oréal-UNESCO For Women in Science International Awards, which specifically supports research by postdoctoral women scientists. This year's awardees are: Kaveeta Kaw for Biomedical, Kaitlyn A. Webster for Developmental, Rebecka J. Sepela for Sensory, Georgia Squyres for Microbiology, and Sydney Aten for Physiology...

Scholarship Alert!

The WAAW Foundation (Working to Advance STEM Education for African Women) has opened applications for its 2025 Scholarship Programme, offering financial and leadership support to young African women pursuing undergraduate degrees in STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) fields. This scholarship helps close the gender gap in STEM by empowering academically talented women to thrive in science and technology careers—while serving as mentors for the next generation of innovators across Africa. Deadline is on December 19th. Make sure to check the requirements and apply by the deadline!

STEM Women Global Congress 2025

GSW (Global STEM Women) is launching their annual congress in Barcelone, Spain on November 19th. STEM WOMEN CONGRESS (SWC) is a big event who works especially to make STEM women visible and to increase the diversity in the STEM companies. ​It is also the space where the results of the Annual Report are presented. With this report we highlight the gaps in the market to accelerate the change for having more girls and women in STEM.
